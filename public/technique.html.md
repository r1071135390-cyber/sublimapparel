# Print Techniques — 20 Decoration Methods Compared

> We run 20 apparel decoration techniques in-house, from sublimation to 3D puff to rhinestone. This page compares cost, MOQ, durability, and best use so you can pick the right one for your design.

**Last updated:** 2025-08-18 · **Author:** Ramon Wang, Sales Director

## Answer capsule
**For an all-over photorealistic print on polyester, sublimation is unbeatable** — colors are permanent, breathable, and never crack. For a small logo on cotton at low MOQ, **DTG or DTF** at 50 pcs. For vintage soft hand at volume, **screen print** at 200+ pcs. For premium logos, **embroidery** — 3D puff adds dimension. For special effects, we run **3D puff, rhinestone, reflective, glow-in-dark, metallic foil, and discharge**.

## Original data: durability vs technique

| Technique | Wash cycles to fade | Hand feel | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sublimation | Indefinite (color is part of fiber) | Soft, breathable | All-over, sportswear |
| Allover digital cotton | 100+ (industrial wash) | Soft, breathable | All-over, premium T-shirts |
| DTG | 50+ | Soft | One-off, short runs |
| DTF | 60+ | Slight film | One-off, low MOQ, vibrant |
| Screen print (plastisol) | 30–50 | Thicker, more durable | Bulk, workwear |
| Screen print (water-based) | 30+ | Very soft | Premium cotton, fashion |
| Embroidery | Indefinite | Textured, premium | Caps, polos, workwear |
| 3D puff | 30+ | Raised | Caps, streetwear |
| Heat transfer | 20–30 | Plastic feel | Names, numbers |
| Reflective | 30+ | Slight texture | Safety, streetwear |
